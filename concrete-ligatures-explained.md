# Concrete Ligatures Explained: What Ligs Do in Reinforcement

Concrete ligatures, often called ligs on site, are the small steel ties that help keep reinforcing bars in the correct shape, spacing and position before concrete is poured. They may look like a simple accessory, but in a footing, beam, column or trench reinforcement cage, they help the steel work as the engineer intended.

If you are comparing ligatures for a project, start with the practical questions first: what ligatures are, what they are used for, which types exist, and how they relate to reo or rebar. This guide answers those questions, then points you to the reinforcing products and support services that help turn a drawing into a buildable steel layout.

## What are concrete ligatures?

A concrete ligature is a pre-bent steel component used to hold longitudinal reinforcing bars together. In construction, the term usually refers to square, rectangular, circular or custom-shaped steel ties that wrap around main bars to form a stable reinforcement cage.

The cage then sits inside the concrete formwork with the right cover, spacing and alignment. Once the concrete cures, the embedded steel helps the concrete resist tensile and shear forces that plain concrete cannot handle well on its own.

Ligatures are different from general tie wire. Tie wire fastens steel in place, but a steel ligature provides the shape and spacing of the cage itself. On many engineered elements, the ligature size, pitch and configuration are shown on the reinforcement schedule or structural drawings.

## What are ligs used for in concrete construction?

Ligs are used wherever reinforcing bars need to stay grouped, spaced and restrained during handling, placement and pouring. They are common in beams, columns, bored piers, strip footings, retaining walls and trench reinforcement.

In simple terms, concrete ligatures help:

- Hold main reinforcing bars in the right position.
- Maintain cage shape during lifting and concrete placement.
- Support the shear reinforcement design shown in the engineering drawings.
- Keep reinforcement spacing more consistent than tie wire alone.
- Reduce the risk of bars shifting while wet concrete is being placed and vibrated.

## Ligatures, trench mesh and reo cages

One common source of confusion is the relationship between ligatures and trench mesh. Trench mesh is a reinforcing mesh product used in narrow concrete elements such as footings and beams. Ligatures are the steel ties or shapes that help hold the reinforcing cage together, especially where bars need to be restrained in a three-dimensional arrangement.

On a residential slab with strip footings, for example, the engineer may specify trench mesh in a particular bar configuration, supported at the correct height with chairs or trench mesh supports. In other details, ligatures may be used to tie top and bottom bars into a cage so the reinforcement stays correctly spaced before the pour.

## How ligature spacing is decided

Ligature spacing, sometimes called pitch, is part of the structural design. It is not simply a neatness preference. In beams and columns, closer ligature spacing can be required where shear loads are higher, where bars need more restraint, or where the design calls for tighter confinement.

For site teams, the safest approach is to read the reinforcement schedule carefully and confirm unclear details before ordering or placing steel. A small misunderstanding in ligature size, spacing or orientation can lead to rework once the inspection happens.

## FAQs about concrete ligatures

### What is a ligature in concrete?

A ligature in concrete construction is a steel tie or pre-bent bar shape used to hold reinforcing bars together in a cage. It helps maintain bar spacing and cage shape before and during the concrete pour.

### What is a ligature used for?

A ligature is used to restrain and position reinforcing bars. In structural concrete, ligatures can support shear reinforcement, hold the cage together, and reduce the chance of steel moving during concrete placement.

### What are trench mesh ligatures?

Trench mesh ligatures are ligatures used with trench mesh or footing reinforcement. They help support narrow reinforcement cages commonly found in strip footings, raft slab beams and edge beams.

### What do Australians call rebar?

In Australia, rebar is often called reo, reinforcing bar or reinforcing steel. You may also hear terms like reo mesh, trench mesh, starter bars and ligs on site.
